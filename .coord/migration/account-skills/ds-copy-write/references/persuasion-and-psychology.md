# Persuasion & Consumer Psychology

Read this before writing so persuasion is engineered, not sprinkled. These are
the levers; choose deliberately and place them where they work.

## The Value Equation — the core of why people buy

Perceived value = (**Dream result** × **Belief it'll work**) ÷ (**Time to
result** × **Effort/sacrifice**).

Raise the top, shrink the bottom:
- **Dream result** — sell the outcome, not the object.
- **Belief in success** — the *unique mechanism* ("360° support"), demos, proof,
  authority. The single biggest lever in a script.
- **Time to result** — emphasize speed/instant ("the moment you put it on").
- **Effort** — emphasize "no effort / nothing to learn."

When copy feels weak, it's usually missing belief (no mechanism/proof) or selling
a feature instead of a dream result.

## LF8 — the 8 life forces (sell the result tied to one)

Deep, hardwired drivers. Tie the product's result to one or more:
1. Survival / live longer, healthier. 2. Safety / avoid fear & danger.
3. Sexual companionship. 4. **Comfort** (physical). 5. Status / superiority.
6. Care for loved ones. 7. Belonging / social acceptance. 8. Enjoyable food &
drink.

Rule from the course: **sell the result, and connect that result to an LF8.**
(Travel pillow → LF4 comfort + LF1 health.) The higher/secondary needs (Maslow's
esteem, self-actualization) layer on top once the base is hit.

## Cialdini's persuasion principles — where to place them

- **Social proof** — reviews with photos/video, "thousands already…", UGC. The
  strongest principle; people copy others when unsure. We're swayed more by
  people *like us*.
- **Authority** — expert/medical framing, "recommended by…", a credible source or
  influencer. The lazy brain wants someone to vouch.
- **Liking** — relatable, similar-looking presenter; genuine compliments; shared
  goal. People say yes to those they like.
- **Reciprocity** — give first (a free tip/guide) before asking; they feel a pull
  to return it. In an ad: teach/entertain before selling.
- **Commitment / consistency** — give them an identity to live up to: "אם אכפת לך
  מ-X, אתה חייב לראות את זה."
- **Scarcity** — limited stock/edition raises desire (supply & demand).
- **Urgency** — a reason to buy *now* (limited-time deal) — but keep it credible.

Artificial-scarcity tactics (use honestly): "few units left", "price about to
rise", "bundle limited to N units", "limited stock".

## Behavioral economics & pricing psychology

People decide emotionally, then justify. Same product, framed differently → very
different response.

- **Price anchoring** — show a higher reference price beside the real one; the
  brain compares, doesn't calculate. (₪229 ̶c̶r̶o̶s̶s̶e̶d̶ → ₪149.)
- **Loss aversion** — losing hurts ~2× more than gaining feels good. Make them
  imagine having it, then imagine the lack. They buy from the pain of missing out.
- **Choice overload** — too many options = no decision. Offer **2–3 clear
  options max** (single / pair / kit).
- **Charm pricing** — ₪149/₪159, not ₪150. Within Israel's impulse range
  (~₪89–₪399) people buy without comparing.
- **Don't be the cheapest** — high price signals quality; too cheap reads as
  "what's wrong with it?" and starves your margin.
- **Discounts need a reason** — holiday, season, launch — and a time limit.

## Direct-response fundamentals (tie it together)

DRM = every ad drives an immediate action. The reader thinks **WIFM** — lead with
what's in it for them. The 4 stages of profitable copy: (1) grab attention, (2)
hold interest (people flee boredom), (3) convince of the value, (4) drive action
with a clear CTA. Interesting content × converting content = the multiplier; one
without the other underperforms.
