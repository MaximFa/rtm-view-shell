# Pricing & Unit Economics

Read this before giving any numbers on margins, pricing, budgets, or breakeven.
Model the full picture *before* launch — most failures are math failures, not
marketing failures.

## The full cost stack per order

Never price off COGS alone. A realistic per-order P&L includes:

- **COGS** (product cost from supplier)
- **Shipping** (to customer; varies hugely by speed/origin)
- **Payment processing fees** (~1.5–3.5% in Israel depending on gateway)
- **Ad cost per order** = ad spend ÷ orders (often the single biggest line)
- **Refund/chargeback cushion** (add 5–10% of revenue from day one)
- **App/subscription overhead** (Shopify plan, apps) amortized
- **VAT** — 18% in Israel; decide whether prices are shown VAT-inclusive (B2C
  standard) and remember you remit it (see `israel-market.md`)

What's left is net profit. If it isn't clearly positive, the product or the
pricing is wrong.

## Margin targets

- **Gross margin:** aim for **60–70%** (price ≈ 2.5–3.5× landed cost) so there's
  room for ads and refunds.
- **Net margin:** **15–25%** is healthy for a well-run store. Classic
  AliExpress-only commodity dropshipping often nets only 2–22% before your time
  — a warning sign to differentiate or move upmarket.
- Products without enough gross margin to absorb ad costs are not viable for
  paid traffic, no matter how "trending."

## Pricing strategies (offer as options)

- **Fixed markup** (e.g. 3× COGS) — simple, consistent; ignores perceived value.
- **Perceived-value pricing** — charge what the customer thinks it's worth;
  requires strong brand, great media, and top-tier service. Higher margin, more
  work.
- **Charm/anchor pricing + bundles** — price points like ₪199, plus
  bundle/quantity offers to lift AOV. Usually combine with one of the above.

## Breakeven ROAS — the number to live by

**Breakeven ROAS ≈ selling price ÷ (gross profit per order)**, or more simply:
the ROAS at which ad revenue exactly covers all costs. Rules of thumb:

- **Breakeven ROAS** typically lands around **2–3×** at normal margins.
- **Scaling threshold:** aim for **3–5×** before pouring budget into a
  creative/product.
- Track **true/blended ROAS** (net of fees, refunds, VAT), not the platform's
  reported number — use TrueProfit / Triple Whale.

## Realistic budget expectations

- **First month ad budget:** roughly **$600–$1,500** split across 1–2 platforms;
  TikTok specifically wants **$1,000–$3,000/month** to test creatives, audiences,
  and offers properly.
- Add product samples, store/theme, apps, and a domain on top.
- A budget too small to test properly is the most common reason beginners
  conclude "dropshipping doesn't work." If the user's budget can't fund a real
  test, say so and suggest saving up or starting leaner (e.g. organic content
  first).

## Build the model

When the user wants numbers, build an actual unit-economics spreadsheet (use the
xlsx skill): inputs for COGS, shipping, fees, target price, expected refund
rate, and ad cost per order; outputs for gross margin, net margin, breakeven
ROAS, and profit at different ROAS levels. Ground inputs in their real figures.
