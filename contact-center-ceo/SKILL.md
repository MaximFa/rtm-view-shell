# CEO — Contact Centre Strategic View — RTM View Shell

This skill covers the perspective of a **CEO** on their organisation's contact centre operation.
The CEO is not a CC expert. They care about the contact centre only to the extent that it
affects customers, revenue, brand, regulatory standing, and cost.

The RTM View Shell, in a CEO's world, is **infrastructure** — like an ERP system.
They will rarely open it. But they need to know it exists, that it governs data access
correctly, and that the CC team can present credible performance data at any moment.

---

## 1. Two types of CEO in the CC world

### Type A — CEO of a company that has a CC

The CC is a support function. The CEO cares about it when:
- Customers complain loudly (social media, press).
- A regulator investigates.
- Costs are too high.
- A major client threatens to leave.
- Competitors offer demonstrably better service.

Their question: **"Is our CC good enough to protect and grow the business?"**

### Type B — CEO of a BPO (contact centre outsourcer)

The CC *is* the business. The CEO cares deeply about:
- Winning new contracts.
- Retaining existing clients.
- Operational efficiency (cost vs price).
- Differentiating via technology and people.
- Workforce scalability.

Their question: **"Is our CC better than the competition, and can we prove it?"**

The RTM View Shell is more strategically visible to **Type B** — it is a client-facing product
and a competitive differentiator ("we give you a live view of your operations").

## 3. CC as a competitive weapon — the CEO's strategic lens

### Service as a brand promise

For many companies, customer service is the only physical point of human contact with the brand.
A CEO who understands this uses the CC not as a cost centre but as a **brand asset**.

```
Brand promise: "We're always there for you."
CC delivery:   Answer 80% of calls in 20 seconds, resolve 75% first time.
Customer outcome: Trust, loyalty, advocacy (NPS).
Business outcome: Lower churn, higher CLV, referrals.
```

### Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) link to CC performance

```
CLV = Average purchase value × purchase frequency × customer lifespan

Churn driver:      Poor service experience → customer leaves.
Retention driver:  Excellent service → customer stays + buys more.

1% reduction in churn rate (from 15% to 14%):
  For 10,000 customers × £500 avg annual value = £500,000 additional retained revenue.

CEO question: "What would a 1-point CSAT improvement be worth in CLV terms?"
CC answer:    Requires linking CSAT data with commercial data — BI project, not RTM.
              RTM Shell provides the CSAT trend; business case built in BI/Finance.
```

### NPS as a business metric

The CEO uses NPS not as a CC metric but as a **company health indicator**:
- NPS >50 = excellent (Apple, Amazon level).
- NPS 30–50 = good.
- NPS 0–30 = average.
- NPS <0 = danger.

A CC that consistently scores CSAT <80% is **destroying NPS** — directly impacting growth.

## 4. Digital transformation — the CEO's agenda for CC

### Why CEOs invest in CC technology

The business case a CEO approves:

```
Current state:
  48,000 contacts/month × £4.82 cost per contact = £231,360/month = £2.78M/year

With digital deflection (chatbot handles 30%):
  33,600 agent contacts × £4.82 = £161,952/month
  14,400 bot contacts × £0.40 = £5,760/month
  Total: £167,712/month = £2.01M/year

Saving: £770,000/year
Investment: £350,000 (platform + implementation)
Payback: 5.5 months
ROI (year 1): 120%
```

The CEO approves this. The CC director delivers it.

### AI / automation — CEO-level understanding

| Technology | What it does | CEO's concern |
|---|---|---|
| IVR / self-service | Deflects simple queries | Does it frustrate customers? |
| Chatbot (rule-based) | Handles FAQ-style interactions | Can it really resolve issues? |
| Conversational AI (LLM) | Natural language query resolution | Data privacy, hallucination risk |
| Agent assist (real-time) | Suggests answers to agents during calls | AHT reduction, training cost reduction |
| Quality AI | Auto-scores 100% of calls | Replaces manual QA sampling |
| Workforce AI (WFM) | Improves forecast accuracy | Reduces overstaffing cost |

The RTM shell is the **display layer** for these technologies' output metrics.
A CEO asking "how is our AI chatbot performing?" needs a widget showing:
- Containment rate (% of bot contacts not escalated to agent).
- Bot CSAT (if post-bot surveys collected).
- Topics handled vs escalated.

## 5. Risk management — what keeps a CEO awake at night

### Reputational risk

```
Scenario: CC delivers poor service → customer complains on social media →
  story picked up by journalist → press coverage → share price impact.

CEO action: Reviews CC CSAT and complaint rate weekly.
RTM shell value: Gives CC director credible, real-time data to defend performance
                 or identify problems before they escalate to press.
```

### Regulatory risk

CEOs of regulated businesses (banks, insurers, utilities, telecoms) face personal liability
for regulatory failures in customer service.

| Regulator | Jurisdiction | What they look for in CC |
|---|---|---|
| FCA | UK (financial services) | Fair treatment of customers, complaint handling |
| Ofcom | UK (telecoms, media) | Silent calls, nuisance calls, complaint resolution |
| FTC | US | Telemarketing rules, TCPA compliance |
| GDPR / ICO | EU / UK | Data protection, call recording consent, SAR response time |
| CMA | UK | Misleading scripts, consumer protection |

**RTM shell governance value for CEO:**
- Permission Groups ensure only authorised staff see customer operation data.
- Audit log proves access controls are in place (GDPR Article 25).
- SSO + 2FA + password policy demonstrates security posture to regulators.
- A regulator asking "who had access to your customer service data?" is answered
  by the audit log export from the RTM shell.

### Data breach risk

A CC handles large volumes of PCI-DSS and GDPR-sensitive data. A breach is:
- Regulatory fine (GDPR: up to 4% of global annual revenue).
- Reputational damage (press, social media).
- Client contract termination (if outsourcer).
- CEO personal accountability.

**RTM shell security posture (CEO talking points):**
- JWT with RS256, refresh token rotation, Redis revocation.
- No customer data stored in the shell itself (it's a display container).
- Audit log of all access events — immutable, INSERT-only.
- SSO integration + MFA enforced.
- Security headers, CSP, HSTS — passes penetration test baseline.

## 6. Workforce strategy — people at CEO level

### The attrition problem — CEO's financial view

```
CC: 300 agents, 28% annual attrition = 84 leavers/year

Cost per leaver:
  Recruitment fee:        £1,500 (job boards / agency)
  HR processing:          £500
  Training (8 weeks):     £2,400 (trainer time + materials)
  Productivity loss:      £1,800 (6 weeks at 50% productivity vs full agent)
  Team disruption:        £300 (management time)
  Total cost per leaver:  £6,500

Annual attrition cost:   84 × £6,500 = £546,000/year

Reducing attrition from 28% to 22%:
  18 fewer leavers × £6,500 = £117,000 saved/year
```

CEO asks: "What is our attrition reduction plan and what will it cost vs save?"

### At-home / hybrid workforce

Post-2020, many CCs moved to at-home models. CEO considerations:

| Factor | At-home benefit | At-home risk |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | -20–30% facilities cost | Tech/security setup cost |
| Talent pool | National / global reach | Management complexity |
| Agent satisfaction | Higher for many agents | Isolation, wellbeing risk |
| Security | Lower physical theft risk | Higher cyber risk (home networks) |
| Regulatory | Flexible | PCI-DSS at home is complex |

RTM shell value: manages permissions and access regardless of agent location.
Supervisors can monitor remote teams the same way as on-site teams.

### Nearshore / offshore strategy

| Location strategy | Cost index | Quality risk | Language risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Onshore (UK/US) | 100% | Low | None |
| Nearshore (Eastern Europe, South Africa) | 40–60% | Medium | Low |
| Offshore (India, Philippines) | 25–40% | Medium-High | Accent, cultural |

CEOs making offshoring decisions weigh cost vs brand risk.
A single viral tweet about poor offshore service can cost more than the savings.

RTM shell multi-tenant model supports multi-site / multi-country deployments.
Each country/vendor could be a separate tenant or BU within one tenant.

## 7. CC as profit centre vs cost centre

### Cost centre model (traditional)

The CC exists to handle contacts as cheaply as possible.
CEO metric: **Cost per contact** (minimise it).
Risk: cutting costs too far → service degradation → churn → revenue loss.

### Profit / revenue centre model (modern)

The CC generates revenue through:
- Retention: talking a customer out of cancelling.
- Upsell: recommending a higher-value product.
- Cross-sell: identifying and offering additional products.
- Collections: recovering outstanding payments.

CEO metric: **Revenue per contact, conversion rate, retention rate**.

```
Retention CC example:
  1,000 cancellation calls/month
  CC retains 35% = 350 customers
  Average retained CLV: £180/year
  Revenue protected: 350 × £180 = £63,000/month = £756,000/year
  CC cost: £50,000/month
  Net value: +£13,000/month

ROI of retention CC: 126% — clearly a revenue centre, not a cost centre.
```

## 9. What a CEO needs from the RTM shell — feature requirements

The CEO rarely opens the shell directly. But they need it to:

### 1. Provide credible data for external commitments

SLA reports sent to clients must match what supervisors see live. The audit log proves
no data was tampered with between live monitoring and reporting. Immutable `INSERT-only`
audit log [AUD-02] is a governance feature, not just a security feature.

### 2. Support regulatory evidence requests

When a regulator asks "who had access to customer service data on [date]?",
the audit log provides a timestamped, IP-traced, user-identified answer.
This is a CEO-level risk mitigation — it can be the difference between a warning and a fine.

### 3. Protect the brand via access controls

The Permission Group model ensures a supervisor in the Sales team cannot accidentally
(or maliciously) access the Complaints team's data. Data segregation is brand protection.

### 4. Scale without rebuilding

Multi-tenant architecture [ARCH-01..10] means adding a new site, a new client,
or a new country is a configuration change — not a development project.
A CEO acquiring a company can onboard their CC as a new tenant in days.

## 11. Glossary — CEO-level terms in CC context

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| BPO | Business Process Outsourcer — company providing CC services to other companies |
| Churn | Customer attrition — rate at which customers stop doing business |
| CLV / LTV | Customer Lifetime Value — total revenue expected from one customer relationship |
| Containment rate | % of self-service / bot interactions resolved without human agent |
| Cost centre | Department viewed as a pure cost — minimise spend |
| Digital deflection | Moving customers from expensive phone channel to cheaper digital channel |
| ESG | Environmental, Social, Governance — non-financial performance reporting |
| IVR containment | % of calls fully handled by IVR without agent involvement |
| NPS | Net Promoter Score — company health indicator based on customer advocacy |
| OKR | Objectives and Key Results — strategic goal-setting framework |
| Payback period | Time for an investment to recover its cost from savings/revenue |
| Profit centre | Department that generates revenue, not just consumes cost |
| QBR | Quarterly Business Review — formal performance review with clients or board |
| Retention CC | CC team focused on persuading customers not to cancel/leave |
| ROI | Return on Investment: (benefit − cost) / cost × 100 |
| SLA contract | Formal agreement on service performance levels, with penalties for breach |
| Total cost of ownership (TCO) | Full lifecycle cost of a system (licences + implementation + support + upgrade) |
| Upsell / cross-sell | Selling higher-value or additional products during a service interaction |
| VoC | Voice of Customer — structured programme to capture and act on customer feedback |
